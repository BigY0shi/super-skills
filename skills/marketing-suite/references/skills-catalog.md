## copywriting

**Goal**: Write marketing copy that is clear, compelling, and drives action.

### Before Writing
Check for product context file first. Then gather:
- Page type (homepage, landing, pricing, feature, about)
- Primary CTA / one action you want visitors to take
- Target audience and their problem
- Key differentiators and proof points
- Traffic source (ads, organic, email)

### Principles
- **Clarity > cleverness** — if you must choose, choose clear
- **Benefits over features** — outcome, not mechanics
- **Specific over vague** — "Cut 4-hour reports to 15 min" not "save time"
- **Customer language** — mirror words from reviews, support tickets
- **One idea per section** — build a logical argument down the page

### Writing Rules
- Simple: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate"
- Active voice: "We generate reports" not "Reports are generated"
- Confident: remove "almost", "very", "really"
- No exclamation points. No buzzwords without substance.

### Page Structure
**Above the fold**: Headline (core value prop) + Subheadline (1-2 sentences expanding it) + Primary CTA

**Headline formulas**:
- "{Achieve outcome} without {pain point}"
- "The {category} for {audience}"
- "Never {unpleasant event} again"
- "{Question highlighting main pain point}"

**Core sections**:
- Social Proof (logos, stats, testimonials)
- Problem/Pain (show you understand their situation)
- Solution/Benefits (3-5 key outcomes)
- How It Works (3-4 simple steps)
- Objection Handling (FAQ, comparisons, guarantees)
- Final CTA (recap value + repeat CTA + risk reversal)

### CTA Copy
**Weak**: Submit, Sign Up, Learn More, Get Started
**Strong**: "Start Free Trial", "Get [Specific Thing]", "See [Product] in Action", "Download the Guide"
**Formula**: [Action Verb] + [What They Get] + [Qualifier]

### Output Format
Organize by section. Annotate key choices. Provide 2-3 alternatives for headlines and CTAs.

## copy-editing

**Goal**: Make existing copy cleaner, clearer, and more persuasive without losing voice.

### Process
1. Read the full piece first — understand the argument
2. Cut anything that doesn't serve the reader or the conversion goal
3. Simplify: replace complex words, shorten sentences, break up walls of text
4. Strengthen: add specificity, improve CTAs, remove passive voice
5. Polish: grammar, consistency, brand voice

### Editing Checklist
- [ ] Jargon that could confuse outsiders?
- [ ] Passive voice constructions?
- [ ] Sentences doing too much at once?
- [ ] Filler phrases ("In order to", "The fact that", "It is important to")?
- [ ] Weak verbs ("is", "are", "has") that could be stronger?
- [ ] Missing benefit statements on features?
- [ ] Unclear CTAs?

### Plain-English Swaps
- "utilize" → "use"
- "leverage" → "use"
- "facilitate" → "help"
- "in order to" → "to"
- "at this point in time" → "now"
- "due to the fact that" → "because"

## content-strategy

**Goal**: Plan content that drives traffic, builds authority, and generates leads — searchable, shareable, or both.

### Before Planning
Gather:
- Business goal for content (traffic, leads, awareness, thought leadership)
- Target customer and their problems
- Questions customers ask before buying
- Current content inventory and what's working
- Competitor content gaps
- Available resources (writers, budget, formats)

### Content Pillars Framework
Organize content into 3-5 pillars:
- **Pillar 1**: Core product/service topic (highest competition, worth it long-term)
- **Pillar 2**: ICP problem category (what they search when they have the problem)
- **Pillar 3**: Adjacent expertise that attracts your ICP
- **Pillar 4**: Competitor / comparison traffic
- **Pillar 5**: Thought leadership / POV

### Content Types by Goal
- **Organic traffic**: SEO-optimized how-to guides, listicles, comparison pages
- **Leads**: Gated guides, calculators, templates, assessments
- **Nurture**: Case studies, ROI calculators, comparison content
- **Brand**: Opinion pieces, original research, podcasts

### Editorial Calendar Structure
- Weekly cadence: [X] blog posts, [Y] social pieces, [Z] emails
- Monthly: 1 premium piece (guide, research, tool)
- Quarterly: Content audit + strategy refresh

### Output Format
Deliver: Content pillars summary + 30-day content calendar + format recommendations per topic

## social-content

**Goal**: Create social content that stops scrolls, drives engagement, and grows audience on platform.

### Platform Guidelines

**LinkedIn**
- Best: Insight posts, personal stories, contrarian takes, step-by-step how-tos
- Format: Short punchy opener → numbered list or short paragraphs → CTA
- Opener should create a "gap" (curiosity, surprise, or pain)
- Max 1-3 hashtags

**Twitter/X**
- Best: Hot takes, threads (use 1/, 2/, etc.), reactions to news, mini-lessons
- Threads: Hook tweet → meat (7-12 tweets) → summary + CTA
- Hook formats: "Unpopular opinion:", "I studied X. Here's what I found:", "{Number} things about X:"

**Instagram**
- Best: Behind-the-scenes, before/after, quote cards, carousels
- Caption: Hook in first line (before "more"), story or context, CTA in last line

**TikTok**
- Best: Educational hooks, "did you know", transformations, hot takes
- First 2 seconds must hook — lead with the payoff
- Scripts: Hook → Context → Content → Takeaway → CTA

### Viral Mechanics
- Disagree with conventional wisdom (backed by evidence)
- Reveal what "everyone knows" but nobody says
- Share personal failure/lesson
- Give away something usually paid

### Output Format
Provide: Platform + format + full post text + posting time recommendation

## ad-creative

**Goal**: Generate high-volume ad copy variations for paid channels.

### Before Writing
Gather:
- Platform (Google Search, Meta, LinkedIn, TikTok, Display)
- Product/offer
- Target audience and pain points
- Top-performing existing ads (if any)
- Character/word limits for the platform

### Google Search Ads (RSA)
- 15 headlines (max 30 chars each): keyword-focused, benefit-led, CTA
- 4 descriptions (max 90 chars each): expand on benefits, include social proof
- Mix: 5 keyword headlines + 5 benefit headlines + 5 CTA/urgency headlines

### Meta Ads (Facebook/Instagram)
- Primary text (125 chars shown, 500 max): hook → problem → solution → CTA
- Headline (27 chars): benefit or curiosity
- Description: expand or add social proof
- Generate 5-10 variations testing: hook style, benefit angle, CTA

### LinkedIn Ads
- Introductory text (150 chars): professional tone, outcome-focused
- Headline (200 chars): specific and role-relevant
- Target: job title, seniority, company size in copy when possible

### Output Format
Deliver variations in a table: Variation | Type | Copy | Character Count

## email-sequence

**Goal**: Build automated email flows that nurture leads and convert to customers.

### Sequence Types
**Welcome (Days 1, 3, 7, 14)**:
1. Confirm signup + deliver value immediately
2. Teach one key concept (no sell)
3. Share a success story / case study
4. Soft CTA: trial, demo, or next step

**Nurture (7-14 emails over 4-6 weeks)**:
- Education → Trust → Problem → Solution → Proof → Objection → Urgency → CTA
- Alternate: value email → soft CTA → value → hard CTA

**Re-engagement (3-5 emails)**:
1. "We miss you" + reminder of value
2. New feature or content they haven't seen
3. "Is this still relevant?" — easy opt-out option
4. Final: "We'll stop emailing unless..." + strong offer

**Onboarding (activation-focused)**:
- Trigger: sign up → immediately guide to first "aha moment"
- Each email = one action, with a clear reason it matters

### Email Structure
- **Subject line**: Curiosity OR benefit OR urgency (never all three)
- **Preview text**: Expand on subject, not repeat it
- **Opening line**: Hook — question, bold statement, or "you" statement
- **Body**: 3-5 short paragraphs. One idea. White space.
- **CTA**: One per email. Button text = action + outcome.

### Subject Line Formulas
- "How [customer] did [result] in [timeframe]"
- "The [topic] mistake killing your [outcome]"
- "Quick question about your [topic]"
- "[First name], your [thing] is ready"

## cold-email

**Goal**: B2B outreach emails that get replies, not unsubscribes.

### Before Writing
Gather: Target persona (title, company type, size), pain point, what you're offering, desired CTA (reply, call, demo).

### Framework: AIDA for Cold Email
- **A**ttention: 1-line subject, 1-line opening that shows you did research
- **I**nterest: Name the exact pain point they likely have
- **D**esire: Show outcome, not features (1-2 sentences max)
- **A**ction: Single soft CTA — "Worth a 15-min call?"

### Cold Email Rules
- Max 5-7 sentences in the body
- Mention something specific to them (recent news, role change, content they published)
- No attachments in first email
- CTA = one easy next step, not "let me know if you're interested"
- Subject lines: 3-6 words, lowercase, specific

### 5-Email Follow-Up Sequence
1. Initial outreach (Day 1)
2. "Just bumping this up" + add one new insight (Day 4)
3. Different angle — different pain point or use case (Day 8)
4. Social proof — mention a similar company you've helped (Day 14)
5. Break-up email: "Should I stop reaching out?" (Day 21)

## email-marketing

**Goal**: Email strategy, deliverability, and automation architecture.

### Strategy Framework
- **Acquisition**: How are you growing the list? (lead magnets, content upgrades, signup CTAs)
- **Segmentation**: Behavioral, demographic, lifecycle stage
- **Automation**: Welcome, nurture, re-engagement, lifecycle triggers
- **Broadcast**: Newsletter, announcements, promotions

### Deliverability Checklist
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC configured
- List hygiene: remove unengaged (90+ days no open) regularly
- Warm up new sending domains gradually
- Monitor bounce rate (<2%) and spam complaints (<0.1%)
- Avoid spam trigger words in subject lines
- Use plain-text version alongside HTML

### Segmentation Signals
- Engagement level (opened in last 30/60/90 days)
- Stage in funnel (trial, paid, churned)
- Industry / company size (if B2B)
- Behavior (visited pricing, used feature X, didn't complete onboarding)

## seo-mastery

**Goal**: Systematic organic growth through search.

### Before Starting
Gather: Domain, main product/service, target audience, current organic traffic (if known), top 3 competitors.

### Keyword Research Process
1. **Seed keywords**: Core product/service terms
2. **Expand**: Use "people also ask", autocomplete, competitor gap analysis
3. **Classify by intent**: Informational (blog/guide), Navigational (brand), Commercial (comparison/review), Transactional (buy/sign up)
4. **Prioritize**: High volume × high intent × achievable difficulty

### Content Architecture
- **Pillar pages**: Broad topic (1,500-3,000 words) — targets head term
- **Cluster pages**: Specific subtopics (800-1,500 words) — target long-tail
- **Internal linking**: Cluster → Pillar always; Pillar → related Pillar when relevant

### On-Page SEO Checklist
- [ ] Target keyword in H1, first 100 words, URL slug
- [ ] Semantic keywords and variations throughout
- [ ] Meta title (50-60 chars): keyword + brand
- [ ] Meta description (150-160 chars): keyword + value + CTA
- [ ] Images: descriptive filenames + alt text
- [ ] Internal links to relevant pages (2-5 per post)
- [ ] Table of contents for long-form (improves jump links in SERPs)

### Link Building Approaches
- Digital PR: newsworthy data studies, original research
- Resource pages: find sites linking to competitors' resources
- Guest posting: write for publications your ICP reads
- Broken link building: find broken links → pitch replacement
- HARO / journalist requests

## seo-audit

**Goal**: Diagnose why a site isn't ranking or traffic dropped.

### Audit Categories

**Technical**
- [ ] Core Web Vitals (LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1)
- [ ] Mobile-friendly
- [ ] Crawl errors in Google Search Console
- [ ] Sitemap submitted and clean
- [ ] No orphan pages (pages with no internal links)
- [ ] Canonical tags correct
- [ ] HTTPS everywhere, no mixed content

**On-Page**
- [ ] Duplicate title tags or meta descriptions
- [ ] Missing H1 or multiple H1s
- [ ] Thin content (< 300 words on indexed pages)
- [ ] Keyword cannibalization (two pages targeting same keyword)

**Content Quality**
- [ ] AI-generated content flags
- [ ] Content matches search intent
- [ ] E-E-A-T signals (author expertise, citations, recency)

**Links**
- [ ] Toxic backlinks (disavow if needed)
- [ ] Internal linking structure
- [ ] Broken internal links

### Output Format
Prioritized list: Critical → High → Medium → Low issues + recommended fixes

## programmatic-seo

**Goal**: Create SEO-optimized pages at scale using templates and structured data.

### pSEO Page Types
- **Location + keyword**: "[Service] in [City]"
- **Integration pages**: "[Tool] + [Tool] integration"
- **Comparison/alternatives**: "[Competitor] alternative", "[Product] vs [Product]"
- **Use case pages**: "[Product] for [industry/role]"
- **Directory/database**: "[Category] directory"

### Implementation Process
1. **Identify template pattern**: What's the repeated structure?
2. **Find the data source**: CSV, API, database, scraped data
3. **Write the template**: Variables in {{ brackets }}, fixed evergreen content
4. **Generate at scale**: 100-10,000+ pages from data
5. **Quality check**: Ensure no thin content, unique enough per page
6. **Internal linking**: Hub page → all location/integration pages

### Quality Standards for pSEO
- Each page must have genuine unique value beyond just swapping variables
- Include local or specific data when available
- Add UGC (reviews, ratings) if possible
- Keep pages above 300 words minimum

## schema-markup

**Goal**: Add structured data to get rich results in Google SERPs.

### High-Value Schema Types
- **FAQ**: Get expanded FAQ results — great for blog posts, landing pages
- **HowTo**: Step-by-step rich results
- **Product**: Price, availability, reviews in SERPs
- **Review/AggregateRating**: Star ratings
- **Article**: Author, publish date, headline
- **BreadcrumbList**: Navigation path in SERPs
- **Organization/LocalBusiness**: Knowledge panel info
- **SoftwareApplication**: App store ratings, OS, price

### Implementation
Always use JSON-LD in `<script type="application/ld+json">` in `<head>`. Avoid microdata.

### FAQ Schema Template
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [{
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "Question text here?",
    "acceptedAnswer": {
      "@type": "Answer",
      "text": "Answer text here."
    }
  }]
}
```

### Validation
Test at: Google Rich Results Test, Schema.org Validator

## ai-seo

**Goal**: Get cited and ranked by AI search engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Gemini).

### AI SEO Principles
- **Be the definitive answer**: Cover the topic more completely than anyone else
- **Structure for extraction**: Use clear headers, bullet lists, definition-first answers
- **Cite sources**: LLMs trust content that cites authoritative sources
- **Answer directly**: Lead with the answer, then explain — inverted pyramid
- **FAQ format**: Directly target common questions your ICP asks AIs

### Content Patterns That Get Cited
- "What is [X]?" definitions with clear, scannable answers
- Comparison tables: "[A] vs [B] vs [C]"
- Step-by-step guides with numbered lists
- Statistics and data with source citations
- Expert quotes and opinions

### Platform-Specific Signals
- **ChatGPT/Bing**: Prioritizes authoritative domains, structured content, Bing-indexed pages
- **Perplexity**: Rewards recently updated content, specific data, niche authority
- **Google AI Overviews**: Rewards E-E-A-T, FAQ schema, featured snippet-style content

## page-cro

**Goal**: Increase the conversion rate of any marketing page.

### Diagnostic Framework
Before recommending changes, diagnose the problem:
- Is this a **traffic quality** problem? (wrong visitors)
- Is this a **clarity** problem? (visitors don't understand the offer)
- Is this a **trust** problem? (visitors don't believe it)
- Is this a **motivation** problem? (visitors don't want it enough)
- Is this a **friction** problem? (too hard to take next step)

### Page Elements Hierarchy
1. **Headline**: Most important — does it communicate clear value instantly?
2. **CTA**: Visible? Compelling button text? Above the fold?
3. **Social proof**: Near the CTA, not buried below fold
4. **Hero image/video**: Does it reinforce the value prop?
5. **Benefits section**: Outcomes, not features
6. **Objection handling**: FAQ, guarantees, testimonials

### Quick CRO Wins
- Rewrite headline to be more specific (add a number or outcome)
- Move social proof logos above the fold
- Change CTA text from generic to outcome-specific
- Add a guarantee or risk-reversal element
- Reduce form fields to minimum required
- Add urgency (but only if real)

### Output Format
Prioritized list of changes: Impact (High/Med/Low) | What to change | Why | How to test

## signup-flow-cro

**Goal**: Reduce friction in signup/registration and increase activation.

### Friction Audit Checklist
- [ ] How many fields? (aim for minimum — email + password is enough to start)
- [ ] Social login available? (Google/GitHub reduces friction massively)
- [ ] Is email verification blocking first-use?
- [ ] Password requirements visible upfront?
- [ ] Error messages clear and specific?
- [ ] Progress indicators for multi-step signup?

### Signup Copy
- Headline: Benefit-forward ("Start building in 2 minutes" not "Create your account")
- Below CTA: Trust signals (no credit card, free forever, 10k+ users)
- Error states: Helpful, not punishing

### Activation Trigger
Define your "aha moment" — the action that predicts retention. Design the signup flow to get users there ASAP.

## onboarding-cro

**Goal**: Get new users to the "aha moment" and activate them.

### Onboarding Principles
- Ruthless prioritization: guide users to one valuable action, not a tour
- Show value before asking for setup effort
- Empty states are conversion opportunities — show examples, not blank screens
- Checklists drive completion (progress effect)

### Onboarding Flow Structure
1. **Signup → Immediate value**: Show something useful before asking for setup
2. **Guided first action**: One specific action with clear outcome
3. **Quick win**: Let them feel success in < 5 minutes
4. **Progressive disclosure**: More features reveal as user advances
5. **Day 3/7 email**: Re-engage if they haven't completed key action

### Metrics to Track
- Activation rate (completed key action in first session)
- Day 1, 7, 30 retention
- Time-to-value (how long to first aha moment)
- Drop-off points in flow

## paywall-upgrade-cro

**Goal**: Convert free users to paid.

### Paywall Types
- **Soft paywall**: Usage limit hit → prompt upgrade
- **Feature gate**: Premium feature → upgrade modal
- **Trial expiry**: Trial ending → conversion screen
- **Hard paywall**: Can't proceed without payment

### Upgrade Modal Structure
1. **Benefit headline**: What they get by upgrading (not "Upgrade to Pro")
2. **Feature comparison**: Brief table or list of what's included
3. **Social proof**: "[N] teams use Pro" or a testimonial
4. **Price with framing**: Show monthly, but charge annually by default
5. **Primary CTA**: "Upgrade to Pro" + risk reversal ("Cancel anytime")
6. **Secondary link**: "Learn more" for fence-sitters

### Psychological Levers
- Loss aversion: Frame as losing access vs gaining features
- Social proof: Show upgrade activity ("1,247 teams upgraded this month")
- Anchoring: Show higher tier first
- Urgency: Trial expiry countdown (real, not fake)

## popup-cro

**Goal**: Capture emails and drive conversions with popups.

### Popup Types and When to Use
- **Exit intent**: Fires on cursor moving to close — best for lead capture offers
- **Scroll-based (50-70%)**: User has shown engagement — good for content upgrades
- **Time delay (30-60 sec)**: Engaged visitor — works for general offers
- **Click trigger**: Most non-disruptive — fires when user clicks a button

### Popup Conversion Rules
- **One offer per popup** — don't confuse with multiple CTAs
- **Clear value exchange**: What do they get for giving their email?
- **Minimal fields**: Email only (name optional)
- **Compelling headline**: "Get [specific thing]" not "Subscribe to our newsletter"
- **Dismiss option**: Always visible — friction kills trust

### Subject Lines for Lead Capture Popups
- "Get the free [guide/checklist/template]"
- "Save [X%] on your first order"
- "Don't leave without this: [specific resource]"

## form-cro

**Goal**: Optimize lead capture, contact, and conversion forms.

### Form Friction Reducers
- Remove every non-essential field (each field costs ~10% conversion)
- Use single-column layouts (easier to scan and complete)
- Labels above fields (not placeholder text — disappears when typing)
- Real-time validation (don't wait until submit to show errors)
- Smart defaults where possible
- Progress bar for multi-step forms
- "Why do you need this?" microcopy for sensitive fields

### Form Copy
- Submit button: Never "Submit" — use action + outcome ("Get My Free Report")
- Privacy line: "No spam. Unsubscribe anytime." near email field
- Heading: Restate the value of completing the form

## ab-test-setup

**Goal**: Design statistically valid A/B tests.

### Before Testing
Define:
- **Hypothesis**: If we change [X], we expect [outcome] because [reason]
- **Primary metric**: One metric that determines winner
- **Secondary metrics**: Guard rails to catch negative side effects
- **Minimum detectable effect**: Smallest improvement worth detecting
- **Traffic split**: 50/50 for simple tests; unequal for risk management

### Sample Size Calculation
Use a sample size calculator. Inputs needed:
- Baseline conversion rate
- Minimum detectable effect (usually 10-20%)
- Statistical significance (95%)
- Statistical power (80%)

Rule of thumb: Need ~1,000 conversions per variant for reliable results.

### Test Duration
- Run for minimum 2 full weeks (captures weekly patterns)
- Don't stop early, even if one variant looks dominant

### What to Test (High Impact First)
1. Headline and value proposition
2. CTA text and placement
3. Hero image or video
4. Social proof placement/type
5. Pricing page layout
6. Form length

## paid-ads

**Goal**: Run profitable paid campaigns across platforms.

### Campaign Structure
- **Campaign level**: Budget, objective, targeting (broad)
- **Ad set level**: Audience segmentation
- **Ad level**: Creative variations (3-5 per ad set minimum)

### Google Ads
- Match types: Start with Phrase/Exact, add Broad with smart bidding later
- Negative keywords: Essential from day 1 — prevent wasted spend
- Quality score: Improve by matching ad copy to landing page
- Smart bidding: Target CPA or Target ROAS once you have 30+ conversions/month

### Meta Ads
- Audience: Start broad with Advantage+ or use detailed interest targeting
- Creative is the primary lever — test 3-5 different angles
- Retargeting: Website visitors (30-day), email list, lookalike audiences
- Budget: Start with $50-100/day to gather data, then scale winners

### Universal Paid Ads Rules
- Never change campaigns mid-flight (let them run 7-14 days minimum)
- One variable changed per test
- Track view-through and post-click conversions
- Pause assets below target CPA/ROAS for 14+ days

## paid-advertising

**Goal**: Full paid media strategy across channels.

### Channel Selection Framework
Match channel to funnel stage:
- **Demand capture** (high intent): Google Search
- **Demand generation** (low intent): Meta, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn
- **Retargeting**: Meta, Google Display, LinkedIn (website visitors + email list)
- **Account-based**: LinkedIn (by company, title)

### Budget Allocation Starting Point
- Google Search: 40-50% (high intent, direct response)
- Meta/Instagram: 30-40% (broad awareness + retargeting)
- LinkedIn: 10-20% (B2B only — expensive CPL but quality)
- Retargeting: 10-15% of total budget

### CAC and ROAS Targets
Set targets before launching:
- What's your LTV:CAC ratio target? (3:1 minimum)
- What's your payback period ceiling? (12-18 months typically)
- Work backward to target CPA/ROAS per channel

## analytics-tracking

**Goal**: Implement complete tracking so every marketing action is measurable.

### Tracking Stack
- **Web analytics**: GA4 (free) or Plausible/PostHog (privacy-first)
- **Tag management**: Google Tag Manager (manage all tags in one place)
- **CRM tracking**: HubSpot, Salesforce, or similar
- **Event tracking**: Custom events for key user actions

### GA4 Setup Checklist
- [ ] GA4 property created and tracking code installed via GTM
- [ ] Enhanced measurement enabled
- [ ] Key conversion events marked as conversions
- [ ] Google Search Console linked
- [ ] Google Ads linked
- [ ] Internal traffic filter set up
- [ ] Cross-domain tracking if needed

### UTM Parameters (Required for All Links)
Format: `utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=brand-awareness`
- Source: Where traffic comes from (google, linkedin, newsletter)

## analytics-attribution

**Goal**: Understand which marketing activities actually drive revenue.

### Attribution Models
- **Last click**: Full credit to last touchpoint — simple but unfair to upper funnel
- **First click**: Full credit to first touchpoint — good for awareness tracking
- **Linear**: Equal credit to all touchpoints — fair but diluted
- **Time decay**: More credit to recent touchpoints — good for short sales cycles
- **Data-driven**: ML-based — best but requires >1,000 conversions/month

### Multi-Touch Attribution
Map the customer journey: First touch → Engagement → Conversion
Track: First-touch source, last-touch source, all touchpoints in between

### Reporting Framework
Weekly metrics:
- Traffic by channel
- Conversions by channel
- CPA by channel
- Revenue by channel

Monthly metrics:
- CAC by channel
- LTV:CAC by cohort
- Attribution model comparison

## marketing-psychology

**Goal**: Apply behavioral science to improve marketing effectiveness.

### Core Principles

**Social proof**: People follow others. Types: testimonials, user counts, logos, reviews, "X people are viewing this"

**Scarcity + Urgency**: Loss aversion is stronger than gain motivation. "Only 3 left" > "Get one now"

**Anchoring**: First number seen anchors perception. Show high price first to make real price feel reasonable.

**Reciprocity**: Give value first (free tool, guide, insight) → makes people more likely to buy

**Commitment + Consistency**: Small yes leads to bigger yes. Free trial → paid is easier than cold purchase

**Authority**: Credentials, awards, PR mentions, expert endorsements signal trustworthiness

**Liking**: People buy from people they like. Humanize brand, show personality, create community

**Framing**: Losses are felt 2x more than equivalent gains. "Stop losing $X" > "Gain $X"

### Copywriting Applications
- Use "because" — even weak reasons increase compliance
- Ask questions — questions engage System 1 thinking
- "People like you" > generic social proof
- Numbers create specificity: "4,382 customers" > "thousands of customers"

## problem-solving

**Goal**: Systematic frameworks for breaking through marketing challenges.

### Simplification Cascades
When campaign/strategy is too complex:
1. List every component
2. For each: "What's the worst that happens if we remove this?"
3. Remove anything where the answer is "nothing much"
4. Keep only what moves the needle

### Inversion Exercise
Instead of "How do I grow signups?" ask:
- "What would guarantee signups NEVER grow?"
- List everything (bad UX, wrong audience, no value prop)
- Now do the opposite

### Meta-Pattern Recognition
When stuck on one campaign, zoom out:
- "What's the pattern in campaigns that worked vs didn't?"
- "What do the 20% of efforts that drove 80% of results have in common?"

### Collision-Zone Thinking
Combine two unrelated concepts to get novel ideas:
- "What if we applied [Netflix model] to [B2B SaaS onboarding]?"
- "What if we ran our email marketing like [a cable news channel]?"

## churn-prevention

**Goal**: Reduce involuntary and voluntary churn.

### Churn Intervention by Trigger

**Usage drop**: "We noticed you haven't [done X] recently" email → highlight a valuable feature they've missed

**Downgrade intent**: Offer pause option, downgrade to lower tier, save offer with discount

**Cancellation flow**: 
1. Ask "why?" (required, but keep options short)
2. Based on reason, show targeted save offer
3. Offer pause > cancel (if applicable)
4. If they cancel: exit survey + win-back sequence

### Dunning (Failed Payment Recovery)
Day 1: "Your payment didn't go through" (friendly, no blame)
Day 4: Reminder + link to update payment
Day 7: Final notice before account pause
Day 14: Account paused + final offer
Day 30: Full cancellation + win-back sequence starts

### Save Offer Hierarchy
1. No offer — just better messaging about value
2. Pause option (1-3 months)
3. Downgrade to free/lower tier
4. Discount (1-3 months)
5. Extended trial on requested feature

## competitor-alternatives

**Goal**: Create SEO-optimized competitor comparison and alternatives pages.

### Page Types
- **"[Product] alternative"**: Target people searching for alternatives to a competitor
- **"[Us] vs [Them]"**: Direct comparison for people considering both
- **"Best [category] software"**: Capture category-level searches

### Page Structure
1. **Intro**: Context — why someone might search this (acknowledge the competitor's strengths)
2. **Quick comparison table**: Key features, pricing, best for
3. **Detailed breakdown**: Each major category with honest assessment
4. **Who should choose [you]**: Be specific — this isn't for everyone
5. **Customer quotes**: If you have customers who switched
6. **CTA**: "See [Your Product]" not "Sign Up"

### Honesty Principle
Don't trash competitors — it reads as desperate. Acknowledge what they're good at, then clearly articulate what makes you different. Credibility > aggression.
